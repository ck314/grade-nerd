Example 1: Connection to Economics (Maximizing Revenue)

  • Concept: In economics, raising the price of a product increases revenue per item, but usually decreases the number of items sold. This trade-off creates a parabolic revenue curve. The Vertex represents the "sweet spot"—the exact price that generates the maximum possible revenue. Using Vertex Form allows an analyst to identify this optimum price instantly without complex calculations.
  • Formula: Revenue(p)=−a(p−h)2+kRevenue(p) = -a(p - h)^2 + kRevenue(p)=−a(p−h)2+k﻿ (Where ppp﻿ is the price, hhh﻿ is the optimal price, and kkk﻿ is the maximum revenue).
  • Example: A market analyst determines that the daily revenue ( RRR﻿ ) for a new gadget, based on its price ( ppp﻿ ), is modeled by the equation R=−10(p−20)2+4000R = -10(p - 20)^2 + 4000R=−10(p−20)2+4000﻿ . What is the optimal price to charge, and what is the maximum revenue?
  • Calculation:
    1. Identify the form: The equation is already in Vertex Form y=a(x−h)2+ky = a(x - h)^2 + ky=a(x−h)2+k﻿ .
    1. Extract hhh﻿ : Inside the bracket is (p−20)(p - 20)(p−20)﻿ , so h=20h = 20h=20﻿ .
    1. Extract kkk﻿ : The term at the end is +4000+4000+4000﻿ , so k=4000k = 4000k=4000﻿ .
    1. Interpret: Since a=−10a = -10a=−10﻿ (negative), this vertex is a maximum.
    • Conclusion: The optimal price is $20, which yields a maximum daily revenue of $4,000.
