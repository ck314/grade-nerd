Example 1: Connection to Economics (Portfolio Risk Management)

  • Concept: In finance, when combining two different assets (like stocks) into a portfolio, their individual price movements (volatility) can be represented as vectors. The angle between these vectors is related to their correlation. The Cosine Law can be used to calculate the total volatility (risk) of the combined portfolio. It shows how diversification can reduce risk, because if the assets are not perfectly correlated, the total risk is less than the sum of its parts.
  • Formula (related to Cosine Law):

    Total Risk² = Risk_A² + Risk_B² - 2 * Risk_A * Risk_B * cos(θ)

    (where θ is the angle representing the inverse of their correlation)

  • Example:

    An investor is combining two stocks. Stock A has a risk value of 8 units. Stock B has a risk value of 10 units. The angle between their performance vectors is 120° (indicating they are negatively correlated - when one goes up, the other tends to go down). What is the total combined risk of the portfolio?

  • Calculation:
    1. Apply the Cosine Law formula:

      Risk² = 8² + 10² - 2(8)(10)cos(120°)

    1. Calculate the values:

      cos(120°) = -0.5

      Risk² = 64 + 100 - 160(-0.5)

      Risk² = 164 - (-80)

      Risk² = 164 + 80 = 244

    1. Take the square root:

      Risk = √244 ≈ 15.62

    • Conclusion: The total risk of the portfolio is 15.62 units. Notice this is less than the sum of the individual risks (8 + 10 = 18), demonstrating the benefit of diversification.
